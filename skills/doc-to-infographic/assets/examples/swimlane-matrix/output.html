The bottom line

The deal moves left-to-right through six phase gates, and ten functions own distinct deliverables along the way. FP&A owns the economic spine — target assessment, model logic, assumption governance, synergy capture, integration budget, and post-close run-rate — while Credit, Treasury, Legal/Reg, HR, Tech and Commercial each validate the risks that drive value. The map below shows the hand-offs and the two real go/no-go decisions, so no function's role is ambiguous at any phase.

What to notice: the two Finance lanes are filled at every phase — FP&A is the only function present start to finish, which is why the playbook hands it the economic spine. Most specialist functions concentrate in Deep diligence (phase 2), where earnings, credit, deposits, compliance, tech, workforce and synergies are all validated at once; their findings then converge into the FP&A decision package at phase 3.

How to read the gate labels

Each column header carries its phase number, name, and the decision gate that closes it (e.g. phase 1 closes with continue / narrow / pause). Empty cells are deliberate: the source assigns no distinct deliverable to that function in that phase, so the slot is shown unfilled rather than padded with an invented task.

What to notice: only phases 1 and 3 are diamonds — these are where the deal can change direction (narrow, pause, no-bid, reprice, defer). The other four gates always advance; they just sharpen the focus of the next round. Keeping the decisions inside the flow shows that a no-bid at phase 3 rests on everything validated in phase 2.

A reminder the source carries forward

The playbook treats target data as unproven until tied to source documents, regulatory filings, management reporting and workstream-owner sign-off. Regulatory approval, legal terms, credit marks and capital outcomes are not concluded until supported by counsel, advisors and current data — these ownership marks show who validates, not who has reached a conclusion.

FP&A's five workstreams are the economic spine the others feed into

Across the whole process, FP&A owns the through-line that converts every function's findings into the decision and then tracks it after close.

Target assessment
Score the target and set diligence priorities — drives proceed / pause / prioritize.
Financial modeling
Own inputs, normalized earnings, PPA, EPS, TBV and capital — base, downside, reprice, no-bid support.
Synergy analysis
Own baseline, timing, leakage and sensitivities — value-capture confidence and valuation support.
Integration planning
Own budget, run-rate baseline and spend tracking — close readiness and budget approval.
Post-close tracking
Track benefits, spend, variances and open items — management reporting and corrective actions.
Assumption governance
Every input is labelled source-provided, public, model assumption, placeholder or user-needed — one integrated risk register, one assumption log.

What to notice: these five workstreams (plus the governance discipline) are why Finance fills every column of the swimlane matrix above — they are the spine that the credit, deposit, regulatory, workforce and technology findings all plug into.
